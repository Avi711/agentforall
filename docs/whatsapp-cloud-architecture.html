How a customer's WhatsApp message reaches the bot, and how the reply gets back

Four pictures and the questions asked along the way: the live message path, the one-time connect, who is allowed to do what inside one agent, and how the orchestrator talks to the database. Green is the customer's message and its reply; amber dashed lines are trust boundaries that a secret never crosses.

1 · The live path: inbound is pulled, never pushed into a container

Meta posts every message to one public URL. That URL only writes to the database. The bot's own container asks its orchestrator for work, so nothing on the internet ever has a route into a tenant.

  • Meta retries a webhook for up to 7 days and can deliver twice; the wamid unique index makes every repeat a no-op, and the marker is kept for 8 days.
  • The ack happens after the agent turn finished, so a crash mid-turn redelivers instead of losing the message. A reply that was already written is resent from the plugin's queue, not asked of the model again.
  • Each customer has their own lane: their messages stay in order, and up to eight customers are answered at the same time. One slow conversation never blocks the others.
  • The orchestrator leases at most 50 messages per bot per tick, so one flooded number cannot starve the other bots on the VM.
  • The container never holds a Meta credential. It holds one relay bearer that identifies the bot; the orchestrator decides which number that bot may send from.
  • The relay refuses a send to anyone whose last inbound is older than 24 hours — Meta's rule, enforced by us, not trusted to the model.

2 · Connect once: the client's number, our app, their token

The client never sees Meta's developer tools. Our one app asks Meta for permission to act on their number; Meta hands back a token scoped to that number only.

3 · One agent, two doors: privilege follows who is talking

Not two bots. The same persona and memory answer on both numbers; what differs is which tools exist in the session, decided by the sender's identity before the model sees a word.

  • OpenClaw's sandbox was rejected: it needs a Docker socket inside the tenant container. Denying tools is the stronger control anyway.
  • The deny list names every tool group OpenClaw 2026.8.2 defines, and a test pins the exact list, so a group added by a future release is a deliberate edit, not a silent hole.
  • The relay's rate limit is per calling container, keyed by its network address, so one container cannot use up another's budget, and forged headers or invented bot ids do not buy it new ones.
  • The global cross-channel send flag stays off. The owner's reply into a customer chat is an owner-only tool; the escalation to the owner is a plain Telegram message sent by the orchestrator with the tenant's bot token. No model reads it on the owner's side, so customer text can never become an instruction there.
  • The gateway's webhook endpoint (/hooks/agent) is not opened: a hook-triggered turn skips the per-sender tool policy and would have run with every tool.

4 · Database connections: borrowed or held

Every arrow into Postgres ends at a real server process with its own memory, and a database has only a limited number of them. The pooler lets many callers share those processes; its mode decides whether a caller keeps a connection or only borrows one for a moment.

Way inModeThe connection is yours forListeningBuilt for
pooler host :6543 Transaction one transaction No many short-lived callers, such as serverless functions
pooler host :5432 Session as long as you stay connected Yes long-running servers, such as the orchestrator
db.<project>.supabase.co:5432 Direct as long as you stay connected Yes tools that need the database itself; no pooler limits, no pooler protection
  • A connection is a conversation that keeps state: a transaction in progress, locks being held, a LISTEN subscription. Opening one costs a network handshake, encryption, a password check and a new server process, so programs open connections once and reuse them.
  • Races are settled by Postgres, not by the pooler, and the same way in every mode: the lease locks the rows it takes with FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED, a duplicate webhook hits the wamid unique index, and a number can be bound to one bot only. Each is one statement or one transaction.
  • The listener reconnects on its own after a drop, waiting 1 s and doubling up to 30 s. While it is down the 500 ms poll carries every message, so a lost connection costs speed, never a message.
  • Production today: the orchestrator connects through the session pooler on port 5432, and the listener shares that one connection string; there is no second secret. Measured: through the session pooler a notification crossed between two connections in 67 ms, through the transaction pooler nothing arrived. The orchestrator refuses exactly that one host and port, and falls back to the poll.

5 · The Meta app: what we set up

The Meta app is our registration with Meta, the company behind WhatsApp. It is what lets our site connect a business's number and carry its messages. Set up on 2026-09-10.

  • Profile. Privacy, terms and data-deletion pages, the domain agentforall.co.il, a category and the logo. Meta requires these before it will publish the app.
  • Connect button. Facebook's login script is allowed on our domain, so the “Connect WhatsApp” popup can open there.
  • Connection settings. A signup configuration (ID 1111278448072808) that asks the business for its WhatsApp account and permission to send and receive messages. Built from Meta's WhatsApp template; its tokens last 60 days.
  • Webhook. Meta delivers incoming messages to agentforall.co.il/api/webhooks/whatsapp-cloud, checked with a shared verify token, subscribed to messages, smb_message_echoes and account_update, shown below.
  • Coexistence. The Connect choice a client sees first: keep the number in the WhatsApp Business app and let the bot answer alongside. Meta registers nothing new, sends at 20 messages a second, and tells us each time the owner answers from the app, so the bot stops for that customer.
  • Vercel. Holds the four values the site needs: app ID and configuration ID (public), app secret and verify token (secret).
  • Checked: Meta's test message and a real reply from a tester's phone both reached our server and passed the signature check, then stopped as an unknown number because no bot holds the test number yet. Meta's own test account had to be linked to our app by hand (subscribed_apps); a client's account is linked by the connect flow.

6 · Questions asked along the way

The questions that came up while this was designed and reviewed, each answered in a line or two.

How it works

Is the Postgres queue a hack instead of a message broker?
No. A table with SKIP LOCKED leases is the standard pattern (Solid Queue, pg-boss, Graphile Worker). A broker would add a component and a second failure mode, and we would still need the table for Meta's dedupe.
Is there a library for it?
pg-boss and Graphile Worker exist, but neither can hand a message out only while that bot's plugin is waiting on this VM. Ours is about 200 tested lines.
Is the plugin busy-waiting?
No. It holds one request open for up to 25 s and sleeps until an answer comes. That is long polling, the way Telegram bots fetch updates.
Does the orchestrator poll every 500 ms?
Only as a safety net: one indexed query, and only while a plugin is waiting. With the listener on, a NOTIFY wakes it at once.
Who sends the NOTIFY?
Postgres itself, when the webhook's insert commits. Every listening orchestrator hears it and acts only for bots waiting on it.
What is the inbox listener?
One database connection per orchestrator that only listens for those NOTIFYs. It reconnects on its own, and the poll covers any gap.
What is the token bucket?
A counter per number that refills at Meta's limit of 80 messages a second. When it is empty we answer 429 before Meta has to.
Does it cost more on Supabase?
No. Supabase bills by compute size, not by query. The polling is tiny index lookups, and none at all while no number is connected.
Does it work with several VMs?
Yes, as built. The webhook writes to the shared database, each orchestrator leases only for bots polling it, and SKIP LOCKED settles two racing.
Won't Vercel get expensive? Can it move to Cloud Run?
It is one signature check and one insert per webhook, roughly a dollar or two per million messages. The route is a thin shell over the ingress code, so moving it is easy.

Customers and the owner

What is an escalation?
The bot hands a customer to the owner: the orchestrator sends the owner a plain Telegram message. No AI runs on the owner's side.
How does the bot stop and resume for one customer?
A bot or human flag per customer. In human mode the plugin forwards each message to the owner and the bot stays silent until the owner flips it back.
Could a customer make the bot open a browser or read files?
No. A customer's session has every tool removed except escalation, decided by who is writing before the model sees a word.

Connecting a number

Do we have an OAuth flow with Meta?
Yes, Embedded Signup. The popup returns a 30-second code, our server trades it with the app secret for a token scoped to that client's number, and the orchestrator stores the token encrypted.
Which webhook?
The address Meta calls for every customer message: agentforall.co.il/api/webhooks/whatsapp-cloud. It is set once in the Meta app, subscribed to messages only.

The database

What changed in the database?
Four new tables (numbers, inbox, conversations, sends) and their indexes. Nothing that existed was touched.
What are conversations and sends?
Conversations: one row per customer, with the last message time for the 24-hour rule and who answers, bot or owner. Sends: an audit row per outgoing message, deleted after 90 days.
What are autovacuum and churn?
Churn is rows being inserted, updated and deleted all the time, which leaves old versions behind. Autovacuum cleans them up; on the inbox we made it run sooner.
Why does a database need connections at all?
A connection keeps state between queries, such as a transaction, locks or a subscription, and each one is a process on the server. So programs open a few and reuse them.
What is a pooler?
A middleman that lets many callers share a few real connections. Section 4 shows the three ways in.
Why did you first ask for a second secret?
I assumed production used transaction mode and did not check. It uses session mode on port 5432, so the existing connection string is enough.
Who handles races between two connections?
Postgres, with row locks, unique indexes and transactions. It works the same in every connection mode.

Deploying

What is the Caddy rule?
Caddy is the server's front door to the internet. The rule answers “not found” to anyone outside asking for the new internal addresses; our bots reach them from inside.
Can we deploy to a test environment?
There is none, so it went to production dark: the flag is off and nobody sees the channel until the rehearsal passes.

The Meta app

What is the Meta app?
Our registration with Meta. It lets our site connect a business's WhatsApp number and send and receive its messages.
What is “Connect WhatsApp”?
A button on the bot's page. The client logs in with Facebook in a Meta popup, picks their business number and approves; from then on the bot answers that number.
What does subscribing the webhook to messages mean?
Meta offers about 30 event types. We switched on only messages: customer messages and delivery receipts. Everything else stays off.
Is the verify token the same as the app secret?
No. The app secret is Meta's, shown in the app's settings. The verify token is a password we made up, entered in both Vercel and Meta's webhook form.
Why do tokens expire after 60 days?
Meta's WhatsApp signup template sets it and it cannot be changed. Tokens can be renewed before day 60, so a renewal job comes before any client connects.
Can a client keep the WhatsApp Business app?
Yes, and it is the default. The app stays on the phone and the bot answers alongside. When the owner answers a customer from the app, the bot steps back for that customer.
When does the bot answer that customer again?
24 hours after the owner's last message to them; every owner message restarts the clock. The owner can bring the bot back at once from Telegram. Chosen from what Meta's own Messenger handover, ManyChat and Zendesk do.
Who decides whether the bot or the owner answers?
One switch per customer, written only by our server. The owner flips it from Telegram (no timer), or by answering in the app (24 hours); right before sending, the bot checks it again.
Can a number that moved fully to the bot go back to the app?
Yes, after disconnecting. The app may ask for the two-step PIN we set; showing it to the owner is a follow-up.
Why subscribe to account_update?
A business can disconnect us from inside the WhatsApp Business app. This event tells us at once, so we take the number off the bot and tell the owner, instead of the dashboard showing “connected” until a later message fails.
Why didn't the first real message arrive?
Meta's test account was not linked to our app, only to Meta's own dashboard app. One API call linked it and the next message arrived. Clients' accounts are linked automatically when they connect.
